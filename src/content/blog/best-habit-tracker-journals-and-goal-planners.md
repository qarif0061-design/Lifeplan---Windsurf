## Planner, wall tracker or notepad: which should you choose?

- **Choose a planner** if your goals involve deadlines and weekly tasks (career, study, business).
- **Choose a wall tracker** if your goals are daily habits (exercise, water, reading, prayer, no sugar).
- **Choose a notepad** if you work on several projects at once and need an action plan for each.
- **Combine two** if you have both: for example a planner for weekly goals and a wall calendar for daily habits.

## How to actually stick with a habit tracker

1. **Track 3 to 5 habits, not 15.** Too many boxes turns tracking into a chore.
2. **Make each habit tiny and specific:** "read 10 pages" beats "read more".
3. **Tick the box straight away,** right after you do the habit.
4. **Never miss twice.** One missed day is normal, two in a row starts a new pattern.
5. **Review weekly.** Spend 10 minutes on Sunday checking what worked and adjusting next week's targets.

## FAQ

### What is the best habit tracker journal for beginners?

For most beginners, a simple wall tracker like the [PIOOJK Habit Tracker Calendar](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DJSXRPH4?tag=lifeplans06-20) or a free printable is the easiest start, because there is nothing to set up. If you also want goal planning, the [Clever Fox Planner](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B079GXFR7S?tag=lifeplans06-20) combines both.

### Is an undated planner better than a dated one?

Undated planners let you start any time and skip weeks without wasting pages, which suits people who have abandoned planners before. Dated planners save you the time of writing dates in.

### How many habits should I track at once?

Start with three to five. Once a habit feels automatic, usually after a few weeks of consistency, you can replace it with a new one.

### Are paper habit trackers better than apps?

Neither is better for everyone. Paper is more visible and satisfying to tick; apps send reminders and are always with you. Many people use both.
