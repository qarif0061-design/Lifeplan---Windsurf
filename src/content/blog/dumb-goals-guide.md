SMART has a useful opposite. **DUMB goals** — sometimes written as Doom, Unclear, Monstrous and Blah, or as Dream-like, Unrealistic, Many and Blah — are a way to name the goals that look ambitious and then go nowhere. The exact words vary, but the idea is the same: if a goal is vague, huge, unowned or impossible, it doesn't matter how motivated you feel in week one.

This guide explains the DUMB pattern, shows how it compares with SMART, and gives before-and-after examples you can use to fix a goal you've already written. It is a companion to [examples of SMART goals and not-SMART goals](/articles/examples-of-smart-goals-and-not-smart-goals) and to the shorter [DUMB goals](/articles/dumb-goals) page.

## What DUMB goals are
There is no single official definition, because DUMB is a teaching device, not a formal framework. A practical version is:

- **D — Dream-like or Doomed.** The goal is a wish, or it depends on luck and other people more than on your own actions.
- **U — Unclear or Unmeasurable.** Nobody can tell what "done" looks like, and there is no number to check.
- **M — Monstrous or Many.** The goal is far too big for the time available, or it is actually five goals wearing one coat.
- **B — Blah or By-and-by.** There is no real deadline, no owner, and no reason that would still matter on a tired Tuesday.

If your goal trips over even two of those, it will be hard to keep. SMART is the repair kit: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound. For a full breakdown of those letters, see the [SMART goal abbreviation](/articles/smart-goal-abbreviation) guide.

## Why people write DUMB goals (and why it isn't laziness)
Most DUMB goals start with a real intention. "Get healthier," "be better at my job" and "sort my money out" are honest wishes. They go wrong when we skip the work of turning the wish into a plan:

- we write the outcome we want, not the behaviour we control,
- we copy someone else's target,
- we set the goal on a burst of motivation, using a best-case week as the plan,
- we never add a date, because a date makes the goal feel serious.

Fixing a DUMB goal is usually a rewrite, not a pep talk.

## DUMB vs SMART, side by side
| DUMB pattern | What it sounds like | SMART fix |
|---|---|---|
| Dream-like | "Be successful this year." | Name one result you can influence. |
| Unclear | "Get fitter." | "Walk 30 minutes, four days a week, for eight weeks, and log each walk." |
| Monstrous | "Write a book this month from scratch, with no writing habit." | "Write 400 words on four days a week for 12 weeks." |
| Blah | "I should really save more, sometime." | "Move 150 on payday each month for six months, reviewed on the first Sunday." |

The SMART version is not smaller in spirit. It is simply something you can do and check.

## Before and after: work
**DUMB:** "Improve my performance."
**SMART:** "Reduce my average ticket response time from 24 hours to 18 hours by 31 March, by using saved replies and a daily queue review."

**DUMB:** "Network more."
**SMART:** "Have one 20-minute coffee or call with a colleague in another team every two weeks for the next three months, booked a week ahead."

**DUMB:** "Be a better manager."
**SMART:** "Hold a 30-minute one-to-one with each direct report every two weeks for the next 12 months, with notes of agreed actions, aiming for at least 90% of meetings to happen."

## Before and after: health, money and learning
**DUMB:** "Get in shape for summer."
**SMART:** "Walk for 30 minutes after work on four days a week for the next eight weeks, and tick each walk off in my planner."

**DUMB:** "Save a lot this year."
**SMART:** "Move 200 into a separate savings account on the first of each month for 12 months, and review the balance every quarter."

**DUMB:** "Learn a new language."
**SMART:** "Practise for 15 minutes on five days a week for 12 weeks, plus one short conversation at the weekend, so I can hold a basic exchange on a trip in June."

**DUMB:** "Read more."
**SMART:** "Read 10 pages a day for 60 days, and write down each finished book."

## How to fix a DUMB goal in 10 minutes
1. **Write the current goal exactly as it is.** Don't polish it first.
2. **Circle the DUMB parts.** No number? No date? Too many goals in one sentence? No reason? A target you couldn't defend with your actual week?
3. **Add a baseline and a number.** Where are you starting, and what will you count?
4. **Shrink the first cycle.** Four to eight weeks is enough to test the habit. A year-long monster can wait until the first cycle works.
5. **Put a first action in the calendar this week.** If it isn't scheduled, the rewrite is still only a nicer sentence.

The [SMART goal statement](/articles/smart-goal-statement) formula and the [achievable goals](/articles/achievable-goals) guide are useful at step 4. [Realistic goal setting](/articles/realistic-goal-setting) helps if the target still looks heroic.

## When a "big" goal is still worth keeping
DUMB is not a warning against ambition. A marathon, a career change or a book can all be good goals. They become DUMB when the first version has no path. Keep the destination if it matters, then put a realistic first stage underneath it: a 5 km plan, a course and a portfolio, or a daily word count. The big goal can stay on the wall. The weekly action is what you manage.

## A note on language
Some lists spell DUMB as "Dream, Unrealistic, Monster, Boring," or "Distant, Unclear, Monstrous, Blah." Don't get stuck on the exact words. Use whichever version helps you spot the problem in a goal you actually care about. The point of the exercise is a better sentence and a better week, not a perfect acronym.

If you work with a team or a class, DUMB can also be a gentle way to review other people's goals. Ask "is this unclear, or too big, or missing a date?" rather than "this isn't SMART." The conversation stays practical, and the person still owns the rewrite.

## Common mistakes when "fixing" a DUMB goal
- **Making it tiny and lifeless.** A goal can be realistic and still worth caring about. Don't cut it so far that you no longer care.
- **Changing the wording but not the week.** If the calendar doesn't change, the goal hasn't changed.
- **Fixing five goals at once.** Rewrite one, run it for two weeks, then look at the next.
- **Using DUMB as an insult.** It's a checklist, not a judgement of the person.

## FAQ: DUMB goals
### What are DUMB goals?
They are poorly formed goals: dream-like or doomed, unclear, monstrous or many, and blah — with no date, owner or reason. The term is a teaching contrast to SMART, not an official standard. If a goal is vague, huge or unowned, it is a DUMB goal in this sense.

### How do DUMB goals differ from SMART goals?
SMART goals are Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound. DUMB goals usually fail at least two of those tests. The fix is to add a clear action, a number, a realistic weekly workload and a date.

### Is a big goal always a DUMB goal?
No. A big goal is only DUMB if it has no achievable first stage. Split it, add a number and a date to the first stage, and keep the longer destination as a later milestone.

### What's the fastest way to fix a DUMB goal?
Write it down, add a measure and a baseline, shrink the first timeframe to four to eight weeks, and put the first action in your calendar this week. Then review it on a fixed day.
