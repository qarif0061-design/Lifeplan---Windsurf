Administrative assistants often do work that is essential and hard to describe in a review: keeping a diary moving, catching errors before they travel, making other people's work possible. That can make goal-setting awkward. "Keep things running" is true, and it is not a useful target. This guide gives **SMART goals for administrative assistants** you can adapt — for the inbox, the calendar, processes, stakeholders and your own development — and a simple way to write goals that will stand up in a one-to-one.

## What a good admin goal looks like
A good goal for this role is Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound, and it measures something you can influence. You can't always control how many last-minute requests arrive. You can control response times, error rates, templates, handovers and the habits that keep a busy office calm.

Use this formula: *I will [action] so that [measure] moves from [baseline] to [target] by [date].* The [SMART goal statement](/articles/smart-goal-statement) guide has more templates. Find a baseline for two weeks before you set a number, if you can.

## SMART goals examples: inbox, calendar and requests
1. Respond to internal requests in my inbox within one working day on at least 90% of days for the next 12 weeks, tracked with a simple flag at the end of each afternoon.
2. Reduce the number of scheduling conflicts I have to unpick from 8 a month to 3 a month by 30 June, by sending a written confirmation and a conflict check the same day a meeting is booked.
3. Prepare a draft agenda and pack for recurring leadership meetings at least 48 hours in advance, for the next 16 weeks, and log any late packs.
4. Cut the time I spend hunting for a file or a template by half within 8 weeks, by building a labelled folder structure and a one-page "where to find things" note for the team.
5. Clear or file every item older than five days in my priority inbox by 15:30 each Friday for the next two months.

## SMART goals examples: processes and quality
6. Create or update three process checklists (travel, onboarding, invoice routing, or the equivalent in my office) by 31 May, and use each one on the next five real cases, noting any gaps.
7. Reduce small errors in outgoing documents (wrong date, missing attachment, wrong version) from an average of 6 a month to 2 a month by the end of Q2, by using a two-minute pre-send checklist.
8. Introduce a shared meeting-request form or template by 30 April, and get at least 80% of new requests through it within six weeks of launch.
9. Write a short backup guide for my role by 30 June, covering the weekly rhythm, key contacts and the five tasks that cannot slip, so cover is easier when I am away.
10. Review and tidy the shared drive or shared inbox once a month for six months, with a one-line log of what changed.

Quality goals like these are especially useful in a review, because they show impact beyond "I stayed busy."

## SMART goals examples: stakeholders and communication
11. Send a short written recap (decisions, owners, dates) within a day of every meeting I organise, for the next 12 weeks.
12. Agree a preferred channel and response window with each of my main stakeholders by 31 March, and review it once in mid-year.
13. Give a brief status update to my manager every Friday for the next quarter: done, next, and any risks or bottlenecks.
14. Greet and sign in visitors within two minutes of arrival, and keep the reception log complete, on every day I am on the front desk for the next term.
15. Run a 20-minute "how to book time with X" briefing for new starters within their first two weeks, for every new starter over the next six months.

## SMART goals examples: professional development
Admin careers move when the skill set is visible, not only when the office is calm.

16. Complete a course in spreadsheets, a document tool, or the main system we use, by 30 September, studying two hours a week, and apply one new feature in a live process.
17. Shadow a colleague in an adjacent role (operations, HR, finance or executive support) for two hours a month for six months, and write one lesson after each session.
18. Lead the organisation of one event or off-site by 30 November, from checklist to a short debrief afterwards.
19. Ask for structured feedback from my manager and two stakeholders once a quarter this year, and pick two actions to work on for 60 days.
20. Build a simple portfolio of three improvements I have made (a template, a checklist, a before-and-after time saving) by 31 October, for a progression conversation.

## How to choose and review goals in this role
Pick two or three, not ten: one operations goal, one quality or process goal, and one development goal is a strong set. Agree the measures with your manager, especially if your work is shared or interrupt-driven. A goal that ignores the reality of a busy front desk will not last.

Review weekly in ten minutes: did the habit happen, and did the number move? Adjust if a new project or a staffing change has made the target unrealistic. It also helps to agree, in advance, what "good enough" looks like on a heavy day. If the office has a crisis, the minimum version of an inbox goal might be "every urgent request answered, and a written note of what slipped." That is still a standard. It stops a chaotic day from quietly deleting the goal altogether, and it gives you something honest to take to the weekly review.

## Common mistakes
- **Goals that are just the job description.** "Answer the phone" is the work. A goal should improve a measure, a process or a skill.
- **No baseline.** "Be more organised" cannot be reviewed. Start with a two-week count.
- **Targets you don't control alone.** If six people can change the diary, write a team process goal and an individual contributing habit.
- **Forgetting development.** Process goals keep the office running. A skill goal keeps your career moving.

## FAQ: SMART goals for administrative assistants
### What are good SMART goals for an administrative assistant?
Good goals improve a measure you can influence: response time, scheduling conflicts, document errors, handover quality, or a skill you will use in the role. For example: "Reduce outgoing document errors from 6 a month to 2 a month by the end of Q2, by using a two-minute pre-send checklist."

### How many goals should an admin have?
Two or three is enough: one operations or quality goal, and one development goal, with a third only if there is a clear process project. More than that usually gets lost in the daily flow of requests.

### How do I write goals when my day is mostly interruptions?
Measure what you can design: response windows, checklists, confirmation habits, end-of-week inbox standards and templates that reduce rework. Build a baseline in a normal busy week, not a quiet one, so the target is realistic.

### How can I show my impact in a performance review?
Keep a short monthly log of the numbers (response times, errors, packs sent on time) and three examples of problems you prevented or processes you improved. Evidence collected as you go is more convincing than a list written the night before.
