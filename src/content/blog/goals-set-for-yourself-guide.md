The hardest goals to set are often the ones nobody asks you for. Work will give you targets. School will give you deadlines. **Goals to set for yourself** have to come from you, which is why they get postponed, or written as vague wishes that don't survive the first busy week. This guide gives you 25 personal examples, grouped by area of life, and a simple method for writing ones you'll actually keep.

## How to choose goals for yourself (before you write a long list)
A personal goal is worth setting if it would make your weeks better, not just your year look impressive. Use three questions:

1. **What would I notice in 8 weeks if this worked?** Energy, money, a skill, a calmer evening, a stronger friendship.
2. **Can I name a weekly action?** If you can't, the idea is still a wish.
3. **Will it fit an average week?** Plan for a normal Tuesday, not a perfect holiday week.

Start with one goal, or two at most. A short list you keep will change more than a long list you abandon. If everything feels urgent, the [how to prioritize goals](/articles/how-to-prioritize-goals) guide can help you choose. And if you've been setting targets you never hit, [realistic goal setting](/articles/realistic-goal-setting) is worth reading first.

## Goals to set for yourself: health and energy
1. Walk for 30 minutes on five days a week for the next eight weeks, and tick each walk off in a planner.
2. Be in bed with lights off by 11 pm on weeknights for four weeks, checked each morning.
3. Cook dinner at home at least four nights a week this month, with a simple list written on Sunday.
4. Stretch for 10 minutes every morning for 30 days.
5. Drink a bottle of water twice on every workday for the next three weeks.
6. Take a proper lunch break away from my desk on at least four days a week for the next month.

## Goals to set for yourself: money
7. Move a fixed amount into savings on payday each month for the next six months, in a separate account.
8. Track every expense for 30 days, and review the total every Sunday.
9. Pay an extra amount toward one debt each week for 16 weeks, checked on Friday.
10. Review every subscription by the end of next month, starting with a written list this weekend.
11. Build a one-month emergency buffer within 10 months by saving on the first of each month.

These are planning examples, not financial advice. For a large money decision, speak to a qualified adviser.

## Goals to set for yourself: learning and skills
12. Read 10 pages a day for 60 days, and write down each finished book.
13. Practise a language for 15 minutes on five days a week for 12 weeks, plus one short conversation at the weekend.
14. Finish one online course by a set date, completing at least one module a week.
15. Practise an instrument, a craft or a sport for 20 minutes on four days a week for eight weeks.
16. Write 300 words of a personal project on three days a week for two months.

## Goals to set for yourself: relationships and home
17. Have one phone-free meal on five evenings a week for the next month.
18. Plan a one-hour outing or call with a friend every two weeks for the next three months, booked at least a week ahead.
19. Send a short message to one person I care about every Sunday for eight weeks.
20. Declutter one drawer, shelf or surface every weekend for six weeks.
21. Do a 20-minute house reset on four weeknights a week for the next month.

## Goals to set for yourself: time and focus
22. Plan my top three priorities every morning before opening messages, for the next 30 working days.
23. Keep one 45-minute focus block in the calendar on four weekdays for the next six weeks. The [time-management SMART goals](/articles/time-management-smart-goals-guide) guide has more on this.
24. Take one full day off work and chores each month for the next six months, booked in advance.
25. Do a 10-minute weekly review every Sunday: what worked, what got in the way, and what I'll do next week.

## A simple way to write the goal
Use this sentence:

*"I will [action] [how much or how often] for/by [date], because [reason], and I'll track it by [tick list or weekly note]. I'll review it on [day]."*

Then add two extras:

- **A minimum version.** If the full walk isn't possible, a 10-minute walk still counts.
- **A first action this week.** Put it in the calendar today.

The [SMART goal statement](/articles/smart-goal-statement) and [achievable goals](/articles/achievable-goals) guides will help if the first draft is still too big.

## How to keep personal goals alive
Personal goals have no manager and no client. The review habit is the whole support system.

- Keep all of them in one place, not three apps and a notes file.
- Use a yes/no check for habits and a weekly number for amounts.
- Review for 10 minutes on the same day each week.
- If you miss a week, shrink the goal rather than starting from zero with a new list.

## What to do when life is already full
If your week already feels packed, don't start with a dramatic new identity. Start with a goal that steals very little time, or one that replaces a habit you don't like. A 10-minute walk after lunch is easier than a new gym routine. A Sunday message to one friend is easier than "be more social." A 10-page reading habit is easier than "read 50 books." Once a small goal is automatic, you have a better sense of what else will fit. That is how people with full lives still make progress: they add one thing that works, not five things that look good on paper.

It can also help to decide, in writing, what you will not start this month. A short "not now" list protects the goal you actually chose.

## Common mistakes when setting goals for yourself
- **Copying someone else's list.** Their hours, family and money are not yours.
- **Starting with five goals on a Monday.** One or two is enough.
- **Goals that describe a feeling.** "Be happier" needs a behaviour, such as a weekly outing or a sleep routine.
- **No reason.** If you can't say why it matters, a busy week will push it aside.
- **All-or-nothing rules.** One missed day is not a failed month.

## FAQ: goals to set for yourself
### What are good goals to set for yourself?
Good personal goals are small enough for a normal week and clear enough to check. Walking 30 minutes five days a week, saving a fixed amount on payday, reading 10 pages a day, or having one phone-free meal most evenings are all strong starting points.

### How many personal goals should I set at once?
One or two. Personal goals have to share your week with work, rest and other people. Finish one, then add another.

### How do I stay consistent when nobody is checking?
Make the action small enough for a tired day, put it in the same slot in your calendar, track it in one place and review it weekly. Consistency comes from design, not from waiting to feel motivated.

### What if I keep abandoning the goals I set?
The goal is probably too big, or it isn't actually yours. Shrink the weekly action, shorten the timeframe, and pick something you'd still care about in a quiet week. Then review it on a fixed day for a month before you add anything else.
