A personal portfolio website is one of the best investments a student can make. It shows employers, clients and universities what you can do, not just what you say on a CV. And in 2026 you don't need to be a developer or spend much money to build one. This guide shows how students can build a portfolio website cheaply, usually for the cost of a few cups of coffee per month.

## Why every student needs a portfolio website

- **You stand out.** Most applicants send only a CV. A link to a clean website with real projects is memorable.
- **You control your story.** Social profiles change their rules; your own domain stays yours.
- **It works for every field.** Design, writing, coding, photography, marketing, engineering and even sports coaching all benefit from showing real work.
- **It helps you get freelance work.** A portfolio makes it much easier to win clients on platforms like Fiverr and Upwork.

## What does a student portfolio website cost?

A simple portfolio needs only two things:

1. **A domain name** (yourname.com): usually around $1–15 for the first year.
2. **Web hosting** (where your site lives): budget shared hosting typically costs around $2–4 per month on a longer plan.

That's it. Many budget hosting plans also include a free domain for the first year, a free SSL certificate (the padlock in the browser) and a simple website builder. A realistic first-year budget is **$25–50 total**.

## Step 1: Choose your domain name

Your domain is your online name, so keep it simple:

- Use your real name: **firstnamelastname.com** is ideal.
- If it's taken, try adding a word: **firstnamedesigns.com** or **firstname.dev**.
- Avoid numbers and hyphens, which are hard to remember.
- Prefer **.com**; **.dev**, **.me** and **.io** are good alternatives for tech students.

## Step 3: Pick a website builder

You have three common options:

- **WordPress:** the most flexible. Free themes like Astra or Kadence make clean portfolios easy. Best if you want to grow the site into a blog later.
- **Your host's builder (like Hostinger Website Builder):** drag and drop, fastest to launch, nothing to install.
- **Code it yourself:** if you're a computer science student, a hand-coded site (HTML/CSS or a framework like React) is itself a portfolio piece. You can still host it cheaply or for free on static hosting and connect your domain.

## Step 4: What to put on your portfolio

Keep it to four or five pages:

1. **Home:** your name, one line about what you do ("Computer science student building web apps"), a professional photo and a clear button to your work.
2. **Projects:** 3–6 of your best pieces. For each one: the problem, what you did, the tools you used and the result. Screenshots or photos help a lot.
3. **About:** a short story about you, your skills and what you're looking for (internship, freelance work, graduate study).
4. **CV/Resume:** a downloadable PDF.
5. **Contact:** an email address or simple form, plus LinkedIn and GitHub links.

Quality beats quantity. Three strong, well-explained projects are better than ten unfinished ones.

## Step 5: Launch and promote it

- Add your website link to your LinkedIn, GitHub, Instagram bio and email signature.
- Put it at the top of your CV.
- Submit it to Google Search Console so your name shows up in search.
- Update it every term with new projects.
