"Communicate better" shows up on almost every development plan, and almost nobody can tell you three months later whether it happened. The fix is to make it concrete. These **communication SMART goal examples** turn a vague wish into goals that are Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound, so you know exactly what to practise this week and how you'll know it's working. Below you'll find a simple formula, 18 examples grouped by situation, practical ways to measure communication, and a short monthly review.

## What makes a good communication SMART goal?

Communication feels like a soft skill, so people assume it can't be measured. It can, as long as you measure the *behaviour* rather than the feeling. You can't directly count "clarity," but you can count how many meetings had a written agenda, how quickly you replied to client emails, or how many times you summarised a conversation before ending it.

A good communication SMART goal does three things:

1. **Names one situation.** Meetings, email, presentations, feedback or listening. Not all of them at once.
2. **Counts something you control.** Frequency, response time, length, or a checklist you follow.
3. **Looks for a signal that it helps.** Fewer follow-up questions, fewer missed handoffs, or a better score on a short colleague survey.

## The SMART communication goal formula

Use this sentence pattern:

> **By [date], I will [specific communication behaviour] [how often or how much], measured by [evidence], so that [benefit].**

Example: "By the end of next month, I will send a three-bullet summary within two hours of every client call, tracked in a simple checklist, so that next steps are never in doubt."

Check it against SMART:

- **Specific:** written summaries after client calls, not "follow up more."
- **Measurable:** every call, within two hours, ticked off on a list.
- **Achievable:** three bullets takes five minutes.
- **Relevant:** it solves a real problem (unclear next steps).
- **Time-bound:** reviewed at the end of next month.

If you want more help shaping the sentence itself, see [how to write a SMART goal statement](/articles/smart-goal-statement).

## 18 communication SMART goal examples for work

Change the numbers and dates to suit your role. Start small; it's easier to raise a target than to recover from missing one.

### Meetings

1. **Agendas:** "For the next eight weeks, I will share a written agenda at least 24 hours before every meeting I organise, tracked in my calendar notes, so attendees arrive prepared."
2. **Shorter meetings:** "By the end of the quarter, I will cut my recurring status meeting from 60 to 30 minutes by moving updates to a shared document, and check with the team after four weeks whether anything is being missed."
3. **Closing actions:** "Starting this week, I will end every meeting I lead by reading back each action, owner and due date, and send them in writing the same day."

### Email and written communication

4. **Response time:** "For the next month, I will reply to every client email within one working day, even if only to confirm when a full answer will come, tracked with a weekly inbox check."
5. **Shorter emails:** "By the end of next month, I will keep routine internal emails under 150 words with the request in the first line, and review five sent emails each Friday against that rule."
6. **Clear subject lines:** "For six weeks, every email that needs action will have the action and deadline in the subject line, such as 'Approve budget by Thursday'."

### Listening

7. **Summarise before replying:** "In every one-to-one this month, I will summarise the other person's main point in my own words before giving my view, and note in my journal whether I did it."
8. **No interrupting:** "For four weeks, I will ask a trusted colleague to note when I interrupt in our weekly team meeting, aiming to reduce it to zero by the end of the period."
9. **Questions first:** "In the next ten customer calls, I will ask at least three open questions before suggesting a solution, logged in my call notes."

### Presenting and speaking up

10. **Practice:** "Before each of my next three presentations, I will rehearse out loud twice and time myself, so I finish within the agreed slot."
11. **Speaking up:** "For the next eight weeks, I will contribute at least one idea or question in every department meeting, tracked in a note on my phone."
12. **Simpler slides:** "By the next quarterly review, I will rebuild my standard deck so each slide has one main message and no more than 30 words."

### Feedback

13. **Timely feedback:** "This quarter, I will give specific feedback within two working days of noticing something worth mentioning, positive or corrective, and log each instance."
14. **Asking for feedback:** "By the end of next month, I will ask three colleagues what one thing would make my updates easier to follow, and choose one change to try."
15. **Difficult conversations:** "Within two weeks, I will prepare and hold the conversation I've been avoiding with a teammate about missed deadlines, using written notes on facts, impact and the request."

### Team and cross-team communication

16. **Weekly update:** "Every Friday for the next quarter, I will send my manager a five-line update covering wins, blockers and next week's priority."
17. **Handoffs:** "By the end of next month, I will create a one-page handoff checklist for projects passed to the operations team, and use it on every handoff."
18. **Remote teams:** "For eight weeks, I will post a short written summary in the team channel after every decision made on a call, so colleagues in other time zones see it."

## How to measure communication improvement

This is the question most people get stuck on. Use a mix of the following measurable communication goals signals, and pick only one or two per goal:

- **Counts:** agendas sent, summaries written, feedback conversations held, ideas shared in meetings.
- **Time:** average reply time to emails, minutes per meeting, presentation length against the slot.
- **Checklists:** a yes/no list you tick after each meeting or email (agenda? actions? owner? date?).
- **Outcome signals:** fewer "quick question" follow-ups, fewer missed handoffs, fewer meetings that need a second meeting.
- **Other people's view:** a two-question pulse survey ("How clear are my updates, 1–5?" and "What would make them clearer?") at the start and end of the goal period.

The behaviour count tells you whether you did the work. The outcome signal tells you whether the work mattered. You need both. ## Choosing your SMART communication objectives

Don't take on six goals. Pick one or two, using these questions:

1. **Where do problems actually show up?** Missed actions point to meeting or handoff goals. Confused clients point to email goals.
2. **What have you been told?** Review comments or repeated feedback are the best source.
3. **What's the smallest change with the biggest effect?** Often a single habit, like a same-day summary, fixes many downstream problems.

## A simple monthly review

At the end of each month, spend ten minutes answering:

- Did I do the behaviour as often as I planned? (Check your log.)
- What signal changed, if any?
- What got in the way?
- Keep, adjust or replace the goal for next month?

If you hit the target easily, raise it or move on to a new situation. If you missed it, shrink it. "Summaries after every call" can become "summaries after client calls only" until it becomes automatic.

## Common mistakes with communication goals

- **Too vague:** "Be a better communicator" has no behaviour and no evidence.
- **Measuring only feelings:** "Feel more confident presenting" is fine as a reason, but pair it with something you can count.
- **Too many at once:** working on email, meetings and presenting together usually means none of them change.
- **No check-in date:** without a review, the goal fades quietly by the third week.

## FAQ: communication SMART goals

**How do you measure communication improvement?**
Measure behaviours you control (agendas sent, reply times, summaries written) and pair them with an outcome signal such as fewer follow-up questions or a better score on a short colleague survey taken before and after the goal period.

**What is a good SMART goal for communication?**
"For the next month, I will send a three-bullet summary within two hours of every client call, tracked in a checklist, so next steps are always clear." It names one behaviour, a frequency, a way to track it and a deadline.

**What is an example of a communication smart goal for an employee?**
"Every Friday this quarter, I will send my manager a five-line update covering wins, blockers and next week's priority." It's short, repeatable and easy to check.

**How many communication goals should I set?**
One or two at a time. Choose the situation where communication problems cost you the most, build that habit, then move to the next.

**Can communication goals be part of a performance review?**
Yes. Write them in SMART format and keep a simple log of the behaviour and any signals. That log gives you concrete evidence to discuss at review time. The guide to [SMART goals for employees](/articles/smart-goals-for-employees) has more review-ready examples.
