Good **SMART goal templates** do one job: they stop you from writing a goal that sounds nice but can't be tracked. Instead of a blank page, you get five prompts (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound) that force a vague wish like "get fit" into something you can actually check off.

Below you'll find a free SMART goal template you can copy into a notebook, a document or a printout, a shorter digital outline for your phone, three filled-in examples, and a simple routine for keeping the template alive after day one. Everything here is free to use.

## What a SMART goal template should include

A lot of templates online look busy but skip the parts that matter. A useful SMART goal form has these sections:

1. **Goal in one sentence.** The plain-language version of what you want.
2. **Specific.** What exactly will you do, where, and with whom?
3. **Measurable.** The number or evidence that proves progress.
4. **Achievable.** The time, skills and resources you have, and any gap you need to close.
5. **Relevant.** Why this goal matters now, and which bigger goal it supports.
6. **Time-bound.** The final deadline plus two or three checkpoints.
7. **First action.** The very next step, small enough to do this week.
8. **Obstacles and plan B.** What is most likely to get in the way, and what you'll do then.
9. **Review dates.** When you will look at the template again.

The last three are what separate a template you fill in once from one you actually use. If you want a refresher on each letter, our [complete guide to SMART goals](/articles/smart-goals-complete-guide) goes through them one by one.

## Free printable SMART goal template

Copy this SMART goal outline into a document, print it, or rewrite it on a single notebook page. Leave space under each line to write.

```
SMART GOAL TEMPLATE

Goal (one sentence): ______________________________

S – Specific
What exactly will I do? ____________________________
Where / with whom? ________________________________

M – Measurable
How will I measure progress? _______________________
Target number or result: ___________________________

A – Achievable
Time I can give each week: _________________________
Skills / tools / help I need: ______________________

R – Relevant
Why does this matter now? __________________________
Which bigger goal does it support? _________________

T – Time-bound
Final deadline: ___________________________________
Checkpoint 1: ________  Checkpoint 2: ________

First action (this week): __________________________
Biggest obstacle: _________________________________
Plan B if that happens: ____________________________
Review dates: _____________________________________
```

Tip: put the goal sentence at the very top in big letters. When you glance at the page in two weeks, that's the line you'll read.

## Digital SMART goal template (short version)

On a phone, long forms get skipped. This shorter SMART objectives template fits in a notes app and still covers the essentials:

- **Goal:** I will [action] [how much] by [date].
- **Measure:** I'll track [metric] every [day/week].
- **Why:** This matters because [reason].
- **Next step:** [one action] on [day].
- **Check-in:** every [weekday] at [time].

The first line already contains the S, M and T. The "why" covers relevance, and the check-in keeps you honest about whether the goal is achievable.

## SMART goal template examples (filled in)

Templates make more sense once you see them completed. Here are three filled examples from different areas of life.

### Example 1: Fitness

- **Goal:** Run a 5K without stopping.
- **Specific:** Follow a beginner run/walk plan three times a week at the local park.
- **Measurable:** Track total running minutes per session. Target: 30 minutes of continuous running.
- **Achievable:** I have 40 minutes free on Monday, Wednesday and Saturday mornings. I already own running shoes.
- **Relevant:** I want more energy for work and to keep up with my kids.
- **Time-bound:** Run 5K continuously by 31 January. Checkpoints: 10 minutes continuous by week 3, 20 minutes by week 6.
- **First action:** Do the first run/walk session on Monday at 7 am.
- **Obstacle and plan B:** If it rains, do 30 minutes on the stairs or an indoor workout instead.

### Example 2: Work

- **Goal:** Reply to customer emails faster.
- **Specific:** Answer all support emails in the shared inbox during two set blocks each day.
- **Measurable:** Average first-reply time, checked in the help-desk report every Friday. Target: under 4 hours.
- **Achievable:** The team agreed to protect two 45-minute blocks daily. Saved replies already exist for common questions.
- **Relevant:** Slow replies are the top complaint in recent customer feedback.
- **Time-bound:** Hit the target by the end of the quarter, with a check at week 4 and week 8.
- **First action:** Block the two time slots in everyone's calendar today.

For more workplace ideas, see our [SMART goals examples for work](/articles/smart-goals-examples-for-work).

### Example 3: Study

- **Goal:** Pass the chemistry final with at least a B.
- **Specific:** Revise one chapter per week and do one past paper every Sunday.
- **Measurable:** Past-paper scores logged weekly. Target: 70% or higher on the last two papers.
- **Achievable:** One hour on weekdays after dinner, and the teacher runs a Thursday help session.
- **Relevant:** I need a B in chemistry for the course I want next year.
- **Time-bound:** Exam on 15 December. Checkpoint: 60% on a past paper by 15 November.
- **First action:** Download the past papers and make a revision calendar this weekend.

## How to use a SMART goal planner every week

A SMART goal planner, whether paper or an app, only works if you come back to it. A simple weekly routine is enough:

1. **Same time every week.** Pick a 10-minute slot, for example Sunday evening.
2. **Update the number.** Write down your current measurable result next to the target.
3. **Compare with the checkpoint.** Ahead, on track or behind?
4. **Choose next week's first action.** One concrete step, with a day attached.
5. **Adjust if needed.** If you've been behind for two weeks in a row, change the plan, not your self-respect. Push the deadline, shrink the target, or find more time.

This review step is exactly what the extra letters in [SMARTER goals](/articles/smarter-goals-meaning) are about: Evaluate and Review. A [weekly review](/articles/weekly-review) habit turns any template into a living plan.

## Common mistakes when filling in SMART goal templates

- **Writing a measure you can't actually check.** "Feel more confident" is hard to track. "Speak up in at least one meeting a week" is easy.
- **Listing too many goals at once.** Three active SMART goals are plenty for most people. Five forms half-filled help nobody.
- **Skipping the "why".** When motivation drops in week three, the relevant section is what pulls you back.
- **No checkpoints.** A deadline six months away feels far off until it suddenly isn't. Checkpoints spread the pressure out.
- **Mixing goals and objectives.** A goal is the outcome; objectives are the measurable steps beneath it. Our article on [what a SMART objective is](/articles/what-is-a-smart-objective) explains the difference.

## Paper template or app?

Both work. Paper is great for thinking: writing by hand slows you down and makes you more precise. An app is better for follow-through, because it can remind you of check-ins, show your progress over time and keep every goal in one place.

## FAQ: SMART goal templates

### What should a SMART goal template include?

At minimum: a one-sentence goal, a section for each SMART letter (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound), checkpoints before the final deadline, the first action you'll take, likely obstacles with a plan B, and dates for reviewing progress.

### Is there a free SMART goal template?

Yes. The printable template and the short digital version above are free to copy, print and adapt for personal, school or work use.

### How many goals should go on one template?

One. Use a separate SMART goal form for each goal so every goal gets its own measure, deadline and first action.

### How often should I update my SMART goal template?

Once a week is a good default. Update your measurable number, compare it with your checkpoint and set the next action. Change the plan if you've been behind for two weeks in a row.

### Can I use a SMART goal template for a team?

Yes. Fill in one template per team goal, name an owner for each action, and review it in a regular team meeting.
