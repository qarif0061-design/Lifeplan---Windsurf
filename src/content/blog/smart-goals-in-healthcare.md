Healthcare runs on targets: waiting times, infection rates, handover quality, training hours, patient satisfaction. But a target is not the same as a well-written goal. "Improve patient safety" sounds right and tells a ward team nothing about what to do on Monday. This guide shows how to use **SMART goals in healthcare** to turn broad aims into clear, measurable and time-bound goals, with examples for patient care, safety, quality improvement, teams and professional development.

The examples are illustrations of how to structure a goal, using made-up numbers. Real targets should be based on your own baseline data and organisational policy, and anything touching patient care must follow local clinical governance, professional standards and guidelines.

## Why SMART goals matter in healthcare
Healthcare teams work under pressure, with shifting priorities and a lot of competing demands. Clear goals help because they:

- **Focus effort.** A team can't improve everything at once. A goal picks the one thing that matters most this quarter.
- **Make improvement visible.** When a measure is tracked, a team can see whether a change helped.
- **Support quality and safety.** Many improvement methods, such as audit cycles and quality improvement projects, depend on a measurable aim and a date.
- **Support professional development.** Appraisals and revalidation need evidence of learning and impact.
- **Align the team.** Everyone knows what success looks like.

SMART stands for Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound. In healthcare, the *measurable* and *relevant* parts matter most: choose a measure you can actually collect, and connect it to patient outcomes or experience.

## Writing a good healthcare goal: a simple formula
Use this structure: **In [area], we will [change/do] to move [measure] from [baseline] to [target] by [date].**

Example: "On Ward 4, we will use a standard handover checklist to increase the proportion of handovers that include a documented risk review from 70% to 90% by 30 June."

The baseline matters. Without it, nobody knows whether 90% is ambitious or trivial. Collect a few weeks of data first, if you can. The [SMART goal statement](/articles/smart-goal-statement) guide has more templates for writing the sentence.

## SMART goals in healthcare: patient care and experience examples
1. Increase the percentage of patients who report they were "involved in decisions about their care" from 72% to 85% in the quarterly survey by 30 September, by introducing a short bedside conversation prompt.
2. Reduce average outpatient waiting time for a first appointment from 12 weeks to 9 weeks by 31 December by adding two extra clinic sessions a month.
3. Ensure 95% of discharged patients receive a written discharge summary within 24 hours by 30 June, tracked weekly through audit of 20 records.
4. Respond to 90% of patient complaints within five working days by the end of Q2, tracked on a monthly dashboard.
5. Increase the proportion of patients who receive an assessment of their pain score every shift from 80% to 95% within three months.

## SMART goals in healthcare: safety and quality examples
6. Reduce inpatient falls on the unit by 20% over 12 months by introducing hourly rounding for high-risk patients and a monthly review of every fall.
7. Raise hand hygiene compliance from 85% to 95% in monthly observational audits by 30 September, by adding a visible reminder at every entrance and feedback at team huddles.
8. Cut medication administration delays from 8% to 4% of doses by 31 March through an extra check at the start of every shift.
9. Reduce the number of pressure ulcers acquired in hospital from 6 per quarter to 3 per quarter by the end of the year by completing a skin check on admission for 100% of patients.
10. Complete safety checklist documentation for 98% of procedures by 30 June, with a monthly audit of 30 cases.

## SMART goals in healthcare: wellbeing for staff
Healthcare workers often put themselves last. Goals for wellbeing aren't a luxury, they protect patients too.

23. Get at least seven hours of sleep on four nights out of seven for the next month, reviewed weekly.

## How to set healthcare goals as a team
1. **Pick one priority.** Use data, such as incident reports, audits or patient feedback, to choose where improvement will matter most.
2. **Find the baseline.** Gather two to four weeks of data before setting the target.
3. **Involve the team.** The people doing the work often know the barriers. Their suggestions improve both the goal and the buy-in.
4. **Choose a small change to test.** Try it for a few weeks, measure the effect and adjust. Small tests reduce risk.
5. **Review regularly.** Weekly review for data, monthly for decisions, quarterly for the bigger picture.
6. **Share the results.** Celebrate progress and be open about what didn't work.

## Tracking goals without adding paperwork
Tracking should use data you already collect where possible. Use a simple run chart or table, updated weekly or monthly. Keep one person responsible for each measure and agree how often it will be discussed. The [SMART goal tracking](/articles/smart-goal-tracking) guide covers simple methods.

## Common mistakes with healthcare goals
- **No baseline.** Without it, you can't tell whether the target is meaningful.
- **Measuring what's easy, not what matters.** Choose measures linked to outcomes or experience.
- **Too many goals.** One or two priorities per team are more effective than ten.
- **Targets without a plan.** A number alone doesn't change practice. Add the change you'll make.
- **Blame instead of learning.** Goals work best in a culture where problems are discussed openly.
- **Ignoring staff capacity.** An unrealistic target on a stretched team can backfire.

## FAQ: SMART goals in healthcare
### What are SMART goals in nursing and healthcare?
They are goals written to be Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound, used for patient care, safety, team performance and professional development. For example: "Raise hand hygiene compliance from 85% to 95% by 30 September."

### What is an example of a SMART goal for a healthcare team?
"Ensure 95% of discharged patients receive a written discharge summary within 24 hours by 30 June, tracked weekly through audit of 20 records." It is specific, has a baseline logic, a measure and a deadline.

### How do I choose a realistic target?
Collect baseline data first, then set a target that stretches the team without overloading it. Look at what similar teams achieve, discuss it with staff and review after a few weeks. Adjust if it proves too easy or too hard.

### Can SMART goals be used for professional appraisal?
Yes. Clear, dated goals make it easier to show learning and impact in appraisals and development reviews. Keep a short log of evidence as you go, and follow your own professional body's requirements.
