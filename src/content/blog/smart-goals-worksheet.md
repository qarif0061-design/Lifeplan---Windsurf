A **SMART goals worksheet** turns a fuzzy wish into a plan you can check every week. Instead of staring at "I want to save money" or "I should get fitter," the worksheet walks you through five questions (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound) and then adds the parts most people skip: the first action, the likely obstacles and the dates you'll review your progress.

## What makes a SMART goals worksheet different from a template

A template is a set of blanks. A worksheet is a guided exercise. The difference matters because most goals fail in the thinking, not in the writing. A good SMART goal planning worksheet:

- **Asks questions in order**, so each answer feeds the next one.
- **Forces you to pick a number** before you move on.
- **Checks the goal against your real week**, not your ideal week.
- **Breaks the goal into milestones**, so you know what "on track" looks like at the halfway point.
- **Plans for the bad week** before it happens.

## Free SMART goal setting worksheet (copy or print)

Copy this into a document and print it, or write it out on two notebook pages. Leave a few lines of space under each question.

```
SMART GOALS WORKSHEET

PART 1 – THE ROUGH IDEA
What do I want to change or achieve? ___________________
Why now? _______________________________________________

PART 2 – MAKE IT SMART
S  Specific: What exactly will I do? Where? With whom?
   _____________________________________________________
M  Measurable: What number or evidence proves success?
   Starting point: ________  Target: ________
A  Achievable: Hours per week I can really give: ______
   Skills, tools or help I still need: _________________
R  Relevant: Which bigger goal or value does this serve?
   _____________________________________________________
T  Time-bound: Final deadline: ________________________

PART 3 – SMART GOAL STATEMENT (one sentence)
I will ____________ by ____________, measured by ________.

PART 4 – MILESTONES
25% by ________: _______________________________________
50% by ________: _______________________________________
75% by ________: _______________________________________

PART 5 – ACTION PLAN
First action (within 48 hours): ________________________
Weekly habit that drives the goal: _____________________

PART 6 – OBSTACLES
Most likely obstacle: __________________________________
If that happens, I will: _______________________________

PART 7 – REVIEW
Review day each week: _________
Progress notes: ________________________________________
```

That's the whole SMART worksheet. Seven parts, and none of them takes more than a few minutes once you get the hang of it.

## How to fill out a SMART goals worksheet, step by step

**Step 1: Write the rough idea first.** Don't try to be SMART yet. "Get better at English" or "grow my small business" is fine. The point of Part 1 is to capture what you actually want and why it matters right now.

**Step 2: Make it specific.** Ask "what would I physically be doing?" "Get better at English" becomes "practise speaking English with a tutor and read one English article a day."

**Step 3: Pick a starting point and a target.** This is where most goal worksheets fall apart. Write down where you are today, even if it's zero, and a target you can count. If you're stuck on numbers, these [measurable SMART goals examples](/articles/measurable-smart-goals-examples) show how to measure goals that feel hard to measure.

**Step 4: Test it against your real calendar.** Under Achievable, write the hours you can give in a normal week, not a perfect one. If the goal needs 10 hours and you have 4, either shrink the goal or stretch the deadline.

**Step 5: Connect it to something bigger.** Relevance keeps you going when motivation drops. "Pass my IELTS so I can apply for a master's programme next year" is a stronger reason than "English is useful."

**Step 6: Set the deadline and write one sentence.** Combine everything into a single SMART goal statement. If it doesn't fit in one sentence, the goal is probably two goals.

**Step 7: Add milestones, the first action and plan B.** Milestones tell you whether you're on pace. The first action gets you moving within 48 hours. Plan B stops one bad week from ending the whole goal.

## SMART goal worksheet example (filled in)

Here's a complete worksheet for a common goal, so you can see how the parts connect.

```
PART 1 – Rough idea: Save money for an emergency fund.
Why now: My car repair last month went on a credit card.

PART 2
S  Save into a separate savings account, automatically,
   every payday.
M  Starting point: 0. Target: 3 months of rent saved.
A  I can set aside 10% of each salary. I need to cut two
   subscriptions and cook at home 4 nights a week.
R  Supports my bigger goal of being debt-free and
   less stressed about surprise bills.
T  Deadline: 30 June.

PART 3 – Statement
I will save 3 months of rent in a separate account by
30 June, by moving 10% of every salary automatically.

PART 4 – Milestones
25% by mid-January, 50% by early March, 75% by mid-May.

PART 5
First action: open the savings account and set up the
automatic transfer this weekend.
Weekly habit: Sunday 10-minute money check.

PART 6
Obstacle: an unexpected expense in a month.
Plan B: pause the transfer for that one month only,
then add 5% extra for the next two months.

PART 7
Review day: Sunday evening.
```

Notice how every answer is concrete. Anyone reading this worksheet would know exactly what the person is doing this week, and whether they're on track in March.

## Common mistakes on a SMART goal planning worksheet

1. **Filling it out once and filing it away.** A worksheet only works if you look at it again. Put the review day in your calendar before you finish Part 7.
2. **Skipping the starting point.** Without "where am I now," you can't tell whether a target is ambitious or impossible.
3. **Writing three goals on one sheet.** Use one worksheet per goal. If you have several, rank them and start with the top one or two.
4. **Choosing a measure you can't easily check.** "Feel more confident" is hard to track. "Speak up at least once in every team meeting" is easy.
5. **No plan B.** Life will interrupt you. Deciding in advance what you'll do keeps a missed week from turning into a quit.

## FAQ: SMART goals worksheet

**How do you fill out a SMART goals worksheet?**
Start with the rough idea and why it matters. Then answer each SMART question in order: what exactly you'll do, the starting number and target, the time you really have, the bigger goal it serves and the deadline. Combine those answers into one sentence, add three milestones, a first action within 48 hours and a plan B, and set a weekly review day.

**How long should it take to complete a SMART goal worksheet?**
Usually 15 to 30 minutes for one goal. If it takes much longer, the goal may be too broad. Try splitting it into two smaller goals with their own worksheets.

**How many goals should go on one worksheet?**
One. Each goal needs its own measures, milestones and obstacles. Mixing goals on one sheet makes it hard to see which one is slipping.

**How often should I review my worksheet?**
Weekly is a good default. A short check every week to update your numbers, plus a deeper look at each milestone date, keeps the goal realistic and lets you adjust early.

**Can I use a SMART goals worksheet for work and school?**
Yes. The questions are the same for a sales target, an exam, a fitness goal or a savings plan. Only the measures change.
