"Sell more this quarter" is not a goal. It's a hope with a deadline attached. These **SMART sales goals examples** show how to turn that hope into targets that are Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound, so a rep knows exactly what to do on Monday morning and a manager knows exactly what to coach. Below you'll find a simple formula, examples for individual reps and for sales managers, a way to pick realistic numbers from your own data, and a short weekly review that keeps the goals alive after the kickoff meeting.

## Why sales goals need the SMART treatment

Sales already has a number: quota. So why bother with SMART? Because quota is an outcome, and outcomes are the result of many smaller things you control: calls made, meetings booked, proposals sent, follow-ups done, deals moved forward. A quota on its own tells you where you need to end up. It doesn't tell you what to do this week.

SMART sales objectives fill that gap. They connect the final number to the activities and conversion steps that produce it. They also make coaching fairer. "You need to hustle more" is vague and frustrating. "Your meeting-to-proposal rate dropped from about one in two to one in four, so let's work on discovery calls" is something a rep can act on.

## The SMART sales goal formula

Use this structure for any sales goal:

> **I will [increase/reach/reduce] [specific metric] from [baseline] to [target] by [date], by doing [weekly activity], so that [business reason].**

The baseline matters. Without it you can't tell whether a target is achievable or a fantasy. The weekly activity matters too, because it's the part you actually control.

Quick check before you commit:

- **Specific:** Does it name one metric, one product line or one segment?
- **Measurable:** Can you pull the number from your CRM or a spreadsheet without arguing about it?
- **Achievable:** Is the jump from baseline to target backed by past results or a clear change in activity?
- **Relevant:** Does it feed the team or company target for the period?
- **Time-bound:** Is there a real end date and a midpoint check?

For more help with the wording, see [how to write a SMART goal statement](/articles/smart-goal-statement).

## SMART sales goals examples for reps

These are written as templates. Swap in your own baseline and numbers; the figures below are illustrations, not benchmarks.

### Activity goals

1. **Outbound calls:** "I will make 40 outbound calls per week to new accounts in my territory for the next 8 weeks, blocking 9–11 AM Tuesday to Thursday, so that I build enough early-stage pipeline for next quarter."
2. **Meetings booked:** "I will book 6 qualified discovery meetings per week by the end of this month, up from 4, by adding a second follow-up email to every cold call."
3. **Follow-up discipline:** "By 31 December, every open opportunity in my pipeline will have a next step and date logged in the CRM, checked every Friday afternoon."

### Pipeline and conversion goals

4. **Pipeline coverage:** "I will build open pipeline worth 3x my quarterly quota by the end of week 3 of the quarter, by prospecting 10 new accounts each week."
5. **Meeting-to-proposal rate:** "I will raise my discovery-to-proposal rate from 30% to 40% over the next 90 days by using a written discovery checklist on every call."
6. **Win rate:** "I will lift my win rate on proposals from 20% to 25% this quarter by running a short pre-proposal call to confirm budget and decision-maker."
7. **Sales cycle length:** "I will cut my average sales cycle for small accounts from 45 to 35 days by 31 March by sending a mutual action plan after the second meeting."

### Revenue and deal goals

8. **Quota attainment:** "I will close $90,000 in new revenue by 31 March, which is 100% of my Q1 quota, by keeping pipeline coverage at 3x and reviewing my top 10 deals every Monday."
9. **Average deal size:** "I will increase my average deal size from $4,000 to $5,000 by the end of Q2 by offering the annual plan and an add-on in every proposal."
10. **New product:** "I will close 5 deals that include our new reporting module by 30 June, by mentioning it in every discovery call with existing customers."

### Retention and account growth goals

11. **Renewals:** "I will renew 95% of my accounts due for renewal this half-year by contacting each one 60 days before its renewal date."
12. **Upsell:** "I will generate $20,000 in expansion revenue from existing customers by the end of the year through a quarterly check-in call with each of my top 25 accounts."
13. **Referrals:** "I will ask every customer who closes in the next 3 months for one referral and turn at least 4 referrals into meetings by quarter end."

Notice the pattern: each goal has one number to watch, a date, and a habit that drives it. Pick two or three that address your weakest stage, not all thirteen.

## SMART sales objectives examples for sales managers

Managers own the team number, but their goals should focus on the levers that lift the whole team. For a deeper list, see these [examples of SMART goals for sales managers](/articles/examples-of-smart-goals-for-sales-managers).

1. **Team attainment:** "My team will reach 100% of the Q2 revenue target by 30 June, with at least 6 of 8 reps at 80% of quota or higher."
2. **Coaching cadence:** "I will hold a 30-minute one-on-one with every rep every week this quarter and review one recorded call in each session."
3. **Ramp time:** "New hires will close their first deal within 60 days of starting, down from about 90, by 31 December, supported by a written 30-60-90 day onboarding plan."
4. **Forecast accuracy:** "My monthly forecast will land within 10% of actual closed revenue for 3 months in a row by the end of Q3, by running a weekly deal review on every opportunity above $10,000."
5. **CRM hygiene:** "By the end of this month, 100% of team opportunities will have a close date, amount and next step, checked in the Monday pipeline meeting."
6. **Pipeline generation:** "The team will create $300,000 in new qualified pipeline this quarter, tracked weekly against a $25,000 per-week target."

A good manager goal is one where the manager's own actions (coaching, reviews, hiring, process) clearly change the result.

## How to set measurable sales goals from your own numbers

Most unrealistic sales goals come from picking the target first and the activity never. Work backwards instead:

1. **Start with the revenue target.** Say $60,000 for the quarter.
2. **Divide by your average deal size.** At $5,000 per deal, that's 12 deals.
3. **Divide by your win rate.** If you win roughly 1 in 4 proposals, you need about 48 proposals.
4. **Divide by your meeting-to-proposal rate.** If half of discovery meetings become proposals, you need about 96 meetings.
5. **Spread over the weeks.** Across 12 weeks, that's 8 meetings a week.

Now you can see whether the target is achievable. If 8 meetings a week is far above anything you've done, you either need more activity, better conversion at one stage, or a conversation about the target. Use your own CRM history for these rates; don't borrow numbers from someone else's industry.

## Tracking sales goals every week

A sales goal set in January and checked in March is a goal you've already missed. A short weekly review keeps it honest:

- **Monday (10 minutes):** Check last week's activity numbers against the weekly target. Plan this week's blocks for prospecting and follow-ups.
- **Midweek:** Review the top 5 open deals. Does each have a clear next step and date?
- **Friday (10 minutes):** Update the CRM, note one thing that worked and one thing to change.

Track leading indicators (calls, meetings, proposals) weekly and lagging indicators (revenue, win rate) monthly. If the leading numbers are on track but revenue isn't, look at conversion. ## Common mistakes with sales goals

- **Only setting a revenue goal.** Add at least one activity goal you fully control.
- **Too many goals.** Two or three per quarter is plenty; ten means none get attention.
- **No baseline.** "Increase win rate" means nothing if you don't know today's rate.
- **Ignoring seasonality.** A December target based on a strong October may not be fair.
- **Goals that hurt customers.** Pushing volume at any cost can raise churn later. Balance new revenue goals with a retention goal.

## FAQ: SMART sales goals

### What is a good sales SMART goal?

A good sales SMART goal names one metric, starts from your current baseline, sets a target and date, and includes the weekly activity that drives it. Example: "I will raise my discovery-to-proposal rate from 30% to 40% in 90 days by using a discovery checklist on every call."

### What are examples of SMART sales objectives?

Common ones include weekly call or meeting targets, pipeline coverage (such as 3x quota by week 3), win rate, average deal size, sales cycle length, renewal rate and expansion revenue. Pick the ones tied to your weakest stage.

### Should sales goals focus on activity or revenue?

Both. Revenue is the result you're judged on, but activity and conversion goals are what you control day to day. Pair one revenue goal with one or two activity or conversion goals.

### How often should sales goals be reviewed?

Check activity numbers weekly and outcome numbers like revenue and win rate monthly, with a bigger review at quarter end to reset targets.

### How do I make sales goals measurable?

Use numbers you can pull straight from your CRM: counts (calls, meetings), rates (win rate, conversion), amounts (revenue, deal size) and time (days to close). Write down today's figure before setting the target.
