Gear doesn't reach your goals for you. Consistency does. But the right basics remove excuses, and a clear plan makes sure that new pair of shoes actually gets used. Here are practical picks by goal, followed by a simple way to plan and track your progress.

## Start with the goal, not the gear

Before buying anything, write one specific goal, for example *"Run 5 km in under 30 minutes within 90 days"* or *"Do 20 push-ups in a row by the end of next month."* Then buy only what that goal needs.

## 1. Running shoes (any cardio goal)
Look for a neutral daily trainer with good cushioning and a fit that leaves about a thumb's width at the toe. Replace them every few hundred kilometres.

## 2. Resistance band set (home strength)
Cheap, portable and enough for full-body strength at home. Choose a set with several resistance levels and a door anchor.

## 3. Adjustable dumbbells
They save space compared with a full rack. Check the weight increments and how securely the plates lock.

## 4. Fitness tracker or smartwatch
Heart rate, steps and sleep data turn vague goals into measurable ones.

## 5. Cricket practice essentials
A tennis-ball bat, practice stumps and a rebound net let you train on your own.

## 6. Football and agility kit
A size-5 training ball, cones and an agility ladder cover most drills.

## 7. Yoga or exercise mat
Around 6 mm thickness is comfortable for floor work; a non-slip surface matters most.

## 8. Insulated water bottle
Hydration is the easiest daily habit to track, and a good start for building others.

## Turn gear into a 90-day plan

1. **Set the outcome:** one measurable goal with a deadline.
2. **Break it into weekly targets:** e.g. three runs per week, adding 10% distance each week.
3. **Schedule sessions:** put them on specific days and times, like any other appointment.
4. **Log every workout:** a quick check-off keeps your streak visible.
5. **Review weekly:** what worked, what didn't, and what to adjust next week.
